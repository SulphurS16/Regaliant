# 11–18 November — recording limits and the cost of a draw


## 11–12 November — a protected page can already be stale

C4 makes a stronger promise than ordinary arming. If CPU access has been excluded, the GPU can keep the newest version until the CPU actually needs it. C5 used that contract inside translated code: a store into a held page could skip its normal write marks (`2dd3cb6`). The measured gain in GPU-bound locations was about 0.4–0.6% and the change was kept (`e0e4eaa`). This small specialization later caused a large preparation failure because it changed the identity of the program the driver compiled.

A crash in the game's heap allocator exposed the precise order required when entering C4. Suppose the mirror matches a page at the time of a check. The CPU can write something new before the runtime makes the page read-only. Observing zero *later* faults gives no evidence about that brief interval. If the stale mirror is then held and copied back wholesale on release, that last CPU write disappears. Commit `d2041f7` required a take after arming and admitted pages to a hold only after that check. The symptom appeared during a CPU allocator read far downstream from the bad decision. An ordinary “page is armed now” assertion would have passed.

Arming itself consumed recording time. Moving it behind the graphics CP and queue 0x50 shifted work to an armer thread, improving Castlewall Front by a factor of 1.053, the vista by 1.013 and the swamp by 1.014 in in-run comparisons (`d77176c`). The follow-up gave the armer a 300 microsecond round budget, lower priority and longer quiet periods. When a fault disarmed neighbours in the same tracking word, pages that nobody had actually written had been repeatedly uploaded as hot. The walk A/B still showed 8–17% more uploaded data and was inconclusive on throughput (`8968c6e`). A fit for one stationary frame could become counterproductive during streaming.

Correctness also depended on following GPU writers through different *image* views. A compressed texture can have one mip level encoded by one GPU view and another level written elsewhere. In `8996d1f` the renderer took each mip from the view that had written it and preserved it across re-uploads. The visible reference was soldiers' faces. This predates the Linux minimum-LOD investigation. Establishing which version of a mip exists and limiting which mip a sampler may request are independent pieces of the texture problem.

The translator achieved a larger measured gain when it used the program's actual control-flow structure. A block dominated by another can be nested inside the region entered by its dominator. If an invocation reaches a proven join, it need not repeatedly check a dispatcher guard. In `62faa41` the tiled-lighting pass dropped from 157 to 96 registers. Across swamp compute, register counts were 0.88 of baseline and binaries 0.90. Reversing the order of the in-run comparison produced speed ratios of 1.143 and 1.148. The robust-buffer hardware path for eligible scalar loads added about 1%. The evidence included validator runs on 4,015 and 1,699 modules and still images within the spread of the control arms. This was a material reduction in work performed by every eligible invocation, independent of how quickly the host queued that work.

## 13–15 November — testing the route a player actually takes

A development build that draws correctly is not sufficient evidence about a fresh install. In `db8a247` a player's black window revealed that GPU execution was not the effective player default. In `6301346` shader preparation ran unhosted and generated specializations without C5's held-page bit, while ordinary hosted play requested it. Prepared compute keys and play keys disagreed. One supposedly prepared first session recorded 548 seconds of compute first-use costs. The fix made preparation ask for hosted-equivalent specifications before generating any. The more subtle cross-PC driver refusal on 19 November would show that matching the application's specification was itself only part of compatibility.

A player's one-frame flashes led to a frame watch that divided the screen into cells and counted abrupt changes. Across about 31,600 frames of a 1-1 walk the then-default configuration produced 886 flashes affecting at least eight of 144 cells, 586 affecting twenty or more. Saved frames showed stray sky geometry, black walls with lit characters, and momentary changes in fog and lighting. Running every batch on the main device queue reduced the two counts to 136 and 26 and improved throughput in that comparison. Letting draws wait for pipelines changed the pattern too. The separate compute route missed dependencies where asynchronous pipeline creation had caused a draw to be omitted. Commit `487e262` made single-queue execution the default and made a stored graphics pipeline eligible for inline creation at its first draw within a 12 ms frame budget. With both changes, 77 draws were left out instead of 311. The cell-count detector also saw legitimate camera motion through walls and foliage. Its absolute count is not a count of proven bugs.

The scouts experiment tried to use additional host threads to record queued submissions ahead of the main CP and hand the results back once they could be checked. It needed worker seats, storage-image tracking and gates to decide when to commit the speculative result (`bca9b2a`, `27d428f`). The difficulty was not solely keeping the main thread busy. At Castlewall the front end already used roughly 120 ms of aggregate CPU per flip and the title about 83 ms at 47 fps, with about 21.7 ms of CP/worker work on the critical path. Extra speculative work competed with both. Relative to no scouts, measured throughput fell to 0.84–0.91. The implementation was removed (`d70008e`). Four impossible compute group sizes learned from stale registers also had to be removed from the store. A speculative recording discarded before submission could still spoil future sessions if learning had already persisted its guesses.

## 16–18 November — eliminating work versus moving it to another thread

The November placement study found a more modest and robust opportunity. Multiple buffer ranges in a batch may share pages the executor has already proved current. The page memo in `65ce8a5` required the same mapping, epoch and queue view. It answered 37% of former full placements, matched 89 million checked answers and improved Castlewall turning by a factor of 1.028. Pipeline lookups also acquired a per-thread cache, eliminating a global mutex on the repeated path. One can understand this optimization from the question each hit answers. The record for one buffer need not match an entire new binding, provided the relevant pages and all conditions used to validate them still match.

The dispatcher attempted a much broader rescheduling. Six versions measured between 0.45 and 0.68 of baseline at Castlewall and it was removed in `26fce05`. Many supposedly serial stretches were points at which CPU shader preparation read the products of preceding GPU work. Handing the stretch to another recorder could neither make the values ready earlier nor determine the bindings without reading them. The culling census likewise warned against a quick bypass: the CPU alternative lost static meshes accounting for about 45% of Castlewall commands. The earlier experiment that sent mirror binds to another device queue also came out after two crossover pairs showed level performance (`b2843d6`, `d539031`).

Less invasive observations sometimes saved work immediately. The executor had been taking snapshots of tiny read-only buffers, then binding the mirror instead. In `a9230a6` those unused snapshots were removed except for the copy kernel whose inputs really did come from them. A guard-kernel experiment could eliminate stops for particle arguments yet yield only a 1.017 ratio at Castlewall and about 0.99–1.01 elsewhere (`e703b47`). Another attempt to resolve draw resources on the GPU still lost the device after moving a varying to location 28 had cleared validation errors. The repeated data fault landed at the same address, pipeline lifetimes had been ruled out, and fault reporting began translating mirror addresses back to guest addresses (`adffec4`). A clean stage interface had fixed a real bug without explaining that hang.

Display pacing developed its own evidence. In `9740d2a` the runtime used observed refresh timing to identify variable refresh and paired a frame's simulation step with the flip that actually showed it. On a 60 fps limit at heavy Castlewall the discrepancy off the refresh grid was about 0.8 ms, compared with 4.0 ms for the title's own step. On a 59.94 Hz grid the corresponding numbers were 8.4 and 8.5 ms. Waking the vblank worker when a flip was ready gave 63.0 uncapped flips/s against 63.2 with the fast timer. The display can determine when a frame becomes visible even after the renderer has stopped being its bottleneck.

The Nexus triangles investigation closed this period with a deliberately unresolved result. With a player's save the flashes disappeared when graphics parallel recording was disabled, and almost disappeared when index culls were off or recording slowed (`d3b35c9`). Barriers after every compute-queue dispatch produced zero flashes in 15,434 frames but only 0.63 of baseline throughput at Doran. Narrowing the barriers to particular queues let flashes return (`3d0e88f`, `5bedad6`). A global barrier changes enough scheduling to hide a stale read without identifying the consumer. The [next period](03e-release.md) begins with the draw that finally identified it.
